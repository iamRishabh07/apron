# Justification Guidelines — Full Documentation

## Context

Each task shows two justifications written by previous raters. These are provided as context and reference — not as your final answer. Your job is to write **one single, high-quality final justification**.

---

## The Most Common Mistake: What NOT To Do

**NEVER** submit both previous justifications in the box, even if separated by a line or space.

This is incorrect because it doesn't produce a single, final consensus — it produces two separate arguments.

**Example of an incorrect submission:**

> "@Response 1 is slightly better overall, both answers give correct boolean outputs…"
>
> "@Response 2 is better because it explains the specific validation logic…"

The first part says "Response 1 is slightly better" and the second part says "Response 2 is better." This is contradictory. Never submit two justifications as one.

---

## The Correct Methods

### Option 1: Edit and Improve (fastest method)

1. Read both previous justifications
2. Decide which is stronger or closer to your own reasoning
3. Delete the other one completely
4. Edit and refine the one you kept — add your insights, correct any errors, make sure it perfectly explains your final rating

### Option 2: Write New from Scratch (best if neither is strong enough)

1. Delete **both** previous justifications
2. Write your own justification from scratch in your own words

---

## The Final Result

Regardless of which method you choose, your final submission must always be a **single, cohesive block of text**. It should read as one clear and complete thought.

**Example of a correct submission:**

> "@Response 1 is the chosen response because it provides a clear step-by-step calculation for converting USD and EUR to EGP, lists the intermediate and final results, and formats them in a simple CSV-style table that's easy to reuse. While it makes a logical error (dividing by 3 unnecessarily), the structure, clarity, and completeness make it more useful and easier to correct than @Response 2. On the other hand, @Response 2 incorrectly applies the same formula to both currencies and fails to provide differentiated exchange rates for the distinct exchange rates, which is a major error in addressing the tasks correctly."
